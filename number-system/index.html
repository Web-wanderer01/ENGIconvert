01

Number System — Basic Concepts

Natural Numbers

Natural Numbers

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, ...}

Positive counting numbers. In many school/competitive mathematics conventions, 0 is not included.

Whole Numbers

Whole Numbers

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}

Natural numbers together with zero.

Integers

Integers

Z = {..., −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}

Positive integers, negative integers and zero.

Rational

Rational Numbers

x = p/q, q ≠ 0

Numbers expressible as the ratio of two integers.

Examples: 1/2, 3/4, −5/7, 2.5
Irrational

Irrational Numbers

x ≠ p/q

Cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers. Their decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Examples: √2, √3, π
Real

Real Numbers

R = Q ∪ Irrational

Includes both rational and irrational numbers.

Prime

Prime Number

Exactly 2 positive factors

A natural number greater than 1 divisible only by 1 and itself.

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13...
Composite

Composite Number

Factors > 2

A positive integer greater than 1 having more than two factors.

4, 6, 8, 9, 10...
Co-Prime

Co-Prime Numbers

HCF(a,b) = 1

Two numbers having no common factor other than 1.

Example: 8 and 15
Twin Prime

Twin Prime Numbers

|p − q| = 2

A pair of prime numbers differing by exactly 2.

(3,5), (5,7), (11,13), (17,19)
Even

Even Number

n = 2k

Every integer divisible by 2.

Odd

Odd Number

n = 2k + 1

Every integer that leaves remainder 1 when divided by 2.

02

Face Value & Place Value

Face Value

Face Value

Face Value = Digit itself

The face value of a digit does not depend on its position.

In 581276, face value of 8 = 8.
Place Value

Place Value

Place Value = Digit × Baseposition

The value of a digit depends on its position in the number.

In 581276, place value of 8 = 8 × 10,000 = 80,000.
Unit Digit

Unit Digit

Unit Digit = N mod 10

The unit digit is the last digit of an integer.

Unit digit of 20197 = 7.
03

Divisibility Rules

Number Divisibility Rule Example
1 Every integer is divisible by 1. 125 ÷ 1
2 Last digit must be even: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8. 248
3 Sum of digits must be divisible by 3. 297 → 2+9+7 = 18
4 Last two digits must be divisible by 4. 2512 → 12 ÷ 4
5 Last digit must be 0 or 5. 2555
6 Number must be divisible by both 2 and 3. 42
7 Subtract twice the last digit from the remaining number. 672 → 67 − 4 = 63
8 Last three digits must be divisible by 8. 5584 → 584 ÷ 8
9 Sum of digits must be divisible by 9. 30555 → 18
10 Last digit must be 0. 500
11 Difference between alternating digit sums must be 0 or a multiple of 11. 217382 → (2+7+8) − (1+3+2) = 11

Unit Digit & Cyclicity

Cyclicity

Unit Digit Cycles

2 → 2,4,8,6

Unit digits of powers repeat in cycles.

2¹, 2², 2³, 2⁴ → 2,4,8,6 → cycle length 4.
Cycles

Common Cyclicities

2:4   3:4   4:2   7:4   8:4   9:2

Use the exponent modulo the cycle length.

Special

Fixed Unit Digits

0, 1, 5, 6 → same unit digit

Any positive power of 0, 1, 5 or 6 retains the same unit digit.

Power of 4

Unit Digit of 4ⁿ

n even → 6   |   n odd → 4
Power of 9

Unit Digit of 9ⁿ

n even → 1   |   n odd → 9
Product

Unit Digit of Product

Unit(ab) = Unit(Unit(a) × Unit(b))

Only the last digits are required when finding the final digit of a product.

Quick Reference — Number Properties

Every prime number greater than 2 is odd. 2 is the only even prime number.
1 is neither prime nor composite. It has exactly one positive factor.
Every integer is rational. Any integer n can be written as n/1.
Every rational and irrational number is real. Real numbers consist of both groups.
Terminating decimals are rational. Example: 0.25 = 1/4.
Repeating decimals are rational. Example: 0.333... = 1/3.
Irrational decimals are non-terminating and non-repeating. Example: √2 and π.
